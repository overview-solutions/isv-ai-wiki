Wireless telecom for village energy

Spectrum layers · leaf / NAN / WAN roles · typical order-of-magnitude figures (not a lab cert)

Village metering and EMS need radios at different stack layers. A LoRaWAN leaf uplink is not a cellular backhaul; Wi‑Fi at the pole shed is not Matter in the home; Starlink can carry MQTT northbound but does not replace neighborhood RF. This page is a decision map — band, range class, throughput class, power, and where each tech usually sits on an ISV-style feeder.

Score at the right layer

Compare leaf → DCU, DCU → DCU (NAN), and site → cloud (WAN) separately. Mixing “LoRaWAN vs LTE vs Starlink” in one cell without a role is how RFPs get confused. Empty / “not verified” cells stay empty — do not invent country-specific licenses from memory.

Honest bounds

Ranges and bitrates below are typical published classes, not guaranteed field results. Terrain, antenna height, duty cycle, and local regulation dominate. Always confirm spectrum rules with the national regulator before a pilot.

Roles on a village feeder

Leaf Meter / sensor → pole

Low duty-cycle uplinks: last-gasp, prepaid events, CT snapshots. Classic: LoRaWAN, sometimes proprietary sub‑GHz. Bluetooth / Matter more often inside a building than on a LV span.

NAN Pole ↔ pole / DCU mesh

Neighborhood area: MeshEMS-style RF mesh, Wi‑Fi mesh, TV White Space bridges, short cellular hops. Higher availability than a single leaf.

WAN Site → cloud / ops

Northbound MQTT/HTTPS: LTE / cellular, fixed wireless, or Starlink when terrestrial WAN is absent. Edge must keep working when WAN dies.

PNT / Broadcast

GPS (and other GNSS) gives time/position — not a data backhaul. AM / FM are one-way broadcast; useful for community info, not AMI command paths.

How ISV usually stacks them

  1. Edge autonomy first — prepaid, relays, and pole EMS keep working when WAN drops (Village Metering overview).
  2. Leaf RF — LoRaWAN or similar for sparse meter/sensor uplinks; RS‑485 / Modbus still common on the panel itself.
  3. NAN — Wi‑Fi LAN at the cabinet; optional mesh / TVWS / proprietary RF between poles (MeshEMS).
  4. WAN — LTE/cellular where coverage exists; Starlink (or other sat) when it does not — carry OpenAMI MQTT / HTTPS northbound.
  5. PNT — GPS for time and map twin geotags; not a control plane.
