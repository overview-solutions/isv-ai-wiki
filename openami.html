OpenAMI

Open Advanced Metering Infrastructure · EnAccess program page · ISV Village Metering thread

An open-source smart metering suite designed with mini-grid developers, not just for them. Goal: replace lock-in to a handful of proprietary vendors with openly documented, field-tested tools operators can own and adapt.

Why OpenAMI

Smart metering is non-negotiable for mini-grids. Tools available to African developers have not kept pace. Most stacks are proprietary, expensive, and tied to one vendor’s roadmap.

Vendor coupling

Opaque SLAs, unstable software, high SaaS fees, and sudden product pivots can disrupt service to whole communities.

No fallback when it fails

Platforms go down; connectivity dies without a local path; vendors change priorities without warning.

Operating risk

Higher opex risk and slower rollout of new mini-grids when the metering layer is closed.

Trust erosion

End users who depend on reliable power lose trust when vending or disconnect cannot run locally.

What it offers working toward

EnAccess describes sector alignments, standards, and a reference AMI architecture that can work across meter OEMs and comms. Results are meant to be collaborative and open-source end-to-end. Deploy locally, keep working during internet outages, and integrate existing OSS such as MicroPowerManager and OpenPAYGO Token. The five items below are capabilities the collaboration is working toward — not a shipped-feature checklist.

1

OEM-agnostic DLMS control

Talk to DLMS (and other protocol) meters from more than one manufacturer. Avoid lock-in to a single proprietary AMI while still using proven metering hardware.

2

Standard AMI features

Load management, remote monitoring, vending, and billing on an open-source stack developers can extend.

3

Local intelligence and vending

A site command station that can manage distribution, local and contingency vending, and post-paid arrangements when backhaul is down.

4

Cloud integration and mirroring

Sync data and state to the cloud when the internet is up — remote oversight without sacrificing local autonomy.

5

Interoperable tokens and tariffs

Token and tariff support that builds on OSS already used in the sector, not a new closed vending silo.

ISV reading (wiki, not the EnAccess page)

Actionable energy telemetry — per-customer reads, credit, tamper, and disconnect semantics operators can bill and act on, not utility-scale archive data alone. Leakage visibility at feeder, branch, and customer so operators can locate loss and theft, not just see aggregate feeder imbalance. The MeshEMS board is aligned hardware for learning that stack in the field.

How to join

EnAccess is building OpenAMI as a community project. Code, documentation, and reference architectures are to be published so developers, implementers, and local partners can use and improve them. The team is sourcing extra hardware, connectivity, and engineering, and looking for users and contributors who can share requirements, test early versions, and bring the stack into new countries.
